1. Introduction to Ind AS 116 (Leases) & Shift from AS 19

Lessee accounting before and after Ind AS 116
Earlier lessee model: Recognise assets and liabilities for finance leases. For operating leases, generally recognise rent expense over the lease term.
Ind AS 116 lessee model: At commencement, recognise a right-of-use (ROU) asset and lease liability, subject to the recognition exemptions.
Case Study: Aviation Industry & Off-Balance Sheet Financing
Aircraft example: Under the earlier operating-lease model, an airline generally recognised rent expense and disclosed future lease commitments.
Under Ind AS 116: The airline recognises its right to use the aircraft and its obligation to make lease payments, subject to the recognition exemptions.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Exam rule: Recognise an ROU asset and lease liability at commencement (para 22). A lessee may elect recognition exemptions for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets (paras 5–8). For a recognised lease, depreciation and interest generally replace rent expense, which can increase EBITDA and reported leverage.

2. Economic Difference Between Rent and Lease

What makes an arrangement a lease?
Rent: A payment described as rent may relate to a lease, a licence, or a service.
Lease: A contract, or part of a contract, that gives the customer the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period in exchange for consideration.
Read the contract terms
Contract title: Apply the same lease test to a licence, service agreement, or equipment hire.
Control of use: Check that the customer can obtain substantially all economic benefits from use and has the right to direct use throughout the period of use.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Exam sequence: Identify the asset → test who controls its use → confirm the period and consideration.

3. Definition of Lease (Ind AS 116 Paragraph 9)

The Statutory Definition
Paragraph 9 Mandate: "A contract, or part of a contract, that conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration."
Embedded Leases: A single master supply or services contract may contain an embedded lease that must be separated and accounted for under Ind AS 116.
Timing of Assessment
At Contract Inception: Assessment whether a contract contains a lease is performed at the inception date.
Reassessment Prohibition: An entity does not reassess whether a contract contains a lease unless the terms and conditions of the contract are modified.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Memory Trigger: [Lease = Identified Asset + Right to Control Use + Period of Time + Consideration]. Reassessment of lease existence occurs ONLY upon contractual modification, not upon mere passage of time or market fluctuation.

4. When Does a Contract Contain a Lease? (ICAI Decision Framework)

The two lease tests
Pillar 1: Identified Asset: Explicitly specified in contract OR implicitly specified at delivery; supplier has NO substantive substitution right; asset must be physically distinct or substantially all capacity.
Pillar 2: Right to Control Use: Throughout the period of use, customer has BOTH: (a) right to obtain substantially all economic benefits from use, AND (b) right to direct the use of the identified asset.
Apply the tests in order
Branch Step 1: Is there an identified asset? (If NO → Service Contract).
Branch Step 2: Does customer get substantially all economic benefits? (If NO → Service Contract).
Branch Step 3: Can the customer direct how and for what purpose the asset is used? If those decisions are predetermined, apply the operation or design tests in para B24(b).
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Exam sequence: Identified asset → substantially all economic benefits → right to direct use. A lease requires all three, plus a period of time and consideration.

7. Substantive Substitution Rights (Ind AS 116 Para B13-B19)

Dual Cumulative Conditions for Substantive Right
Condition (a) Practical Ability: Supplier has practical ability to substitute alternative assets throughout the period of use (e.g. readily available alternative assets without customer consent).
Condition (b) Economic Benefit: Supplier would benefit economically from exercising its right to substitute (benefits of substitution exceed costs of substitution).
ICAI Presumptions & Anti-Avoidance Rules
Customer Presumption (Para B19): If customer cannot readily determine whether supplier has a substantive substitution right, customer shall presume the right is NOT substantive.
Future/Contingent Events Ignored: Evaluation is based on facts at inception. Future events not likely to occur (e.g. future technology upgrade, future price crash) are excluded from evaluation.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
For substitution right to be SUBSTANTIVE, BOTH Practical Ability AND Economic Benefit must co-exist throughout the period of use. If supplier can replace only on breakdown or maintenance, it is protective → Identified Asset exists!

10. Physically Distinct Assets vs Capacity Portions (Para B20)

Physical Distinction Rule
Physically Distinct Portion: A specified floor in a building, a dedicated single fiber in a cable, or a demarcated segment of warehouse space is an identified asset.
Non-Physically Distinct Capacity: A capacity or volume portion (e.g. 20% capacity of a pipeline) is NOT an identified asset UNLESS it represents substantially all capacity.
The 'Substantially All' Threshold
Threshold Criterion: If customer has right to substantially all capacity (e.g. 90-95%+), customer in effect obtains all economic benefits from the entire physical asset.
Minority Capacity Trap: Contracting for 10%, 20%, or 50% capacity without physical segregation is a service agreement, NOT a lease.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Capacity Rule Formula: Portion = Identified Asset IF [Physically Distinct] OR [Substantially All Capacity of the Asset]. If neither condition is met, FAIL → Service contract!

16. Right to Control Use of Identified Asset (Economic Benefits)

Substantially All Economic Benefits (Para B21-B23)
Scope of Benefits: Customer must have the right to obtain substantially all economic benefits from use of the asset throughout the period (e.g. primary output, byproducts, renewable energy credits/RECs).
Benefits within Defined Scope: Evaluated within the contractual scope of use (e.g. if contract limits usage to 100 operating hours/month, evaluate 100% benefits of those 100 hours).
Protective Rights vs Substantive Rights
Protective Clauses: Supplier terms protecting asset value (e.g. routine maintenance, environmental limits, insurance mandates) do NOT prevent customer from enjoying economic benefits.
Direct vs Incidental Benefits: Cash flows directly resulting from operating the asset belong to the customer controlling use.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Economic Benefits Test looks at commercial outputs, sub-leasing proceeds, and incidental byproducts within the agreed scope. Contractual caps (e.g. max 5,000 miles/month) define the scope of the right of use, rather than denying control.

18. Right to Direct the Use of Asset (Para B24-B30)

How and For What Purpose the Asset is Used
Relevant Decision-Making Rights: Right to change what goods/services asset produces, right to change when/where they are produced, and right to change output rate.
Operating / Maintenance Decisions Irrelevant: Decisions on how to operate or maintain an asset (e.g. driving the truck, greasing machines) are service activities, NOT directing use.
Predetermined Use (Two Alternate Tests)
Option A (Operation): Customer has the right to operate the asset (or direct others to operate it) throughout the period without supplier modifying instructions.
Option B (Design): Customer designed the asset in a way that predetermines how and for what purpose it will be used throughout the period.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Golden Distinction: Directing 'How & For What Purpose' = Directing Economic Utility (LEON / Customer). Operating the machine / maintenance = Pure execution (Service). If decisions are predetermined, check if customer operates it or designed it!

24. Inception Date vs Commencement Date & Lease Term Basics

Inception Date vs Commencement Date (Critical Difference)
Inception Date: Earlier of agreement date and commitment date. Used to classify lease (for lessor) and determine discount rate/classification terms.
Commencement Date: Date on which lessor makes underlying asset available for use by lessee. Accounting recognition starts here! (Initial measurement of ROU asset & liability).
Components of Lease Term (Ind AS 116 Para 18)
Component 1: Non-Cancellable Period: Period during which contract cannot be terminated without mutual consent or significant penalty.
Component 2: Extension Options: Periods covered by option to extend IF lessee is reasonably certain to exercise.
Component 3: Termination Options: Periods covered by option to terminate IF lessee is reasonably certain NOT to exercise.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Never book entries on Inception Date! Accounting journal entries are recognized on Commencement Date. Formula: Lease Term = Non-Cancellable Period + Probable Extensions - Probable Early Terminations.

25. Determining Lease Term & Enforceability Test

The Legal Enforceability Test (Para B34)
Mutual Termination Without Penalty: A lease is no longer enforceable when BOTH lessee and lessor each have the right to terminate without permission from the other party with no more than an insignificant penalty.
Lessee-Only Option vs Lessor-Only Option: If only lessor can terminate, lessee has an enforceable period through the full term (contractual right to use is protected).
Economic Factors Assessing 'Reasonable Certainty'
Contractual Factors: Bargain renewal options vs market rates, high termination penalties, return condition obligations.
Asset Factors: Significant customized leasehold improvements made by lessee with remaining useful life exceeding initial term.
Operational Factors: Relocation costs, downtime, strategic importance of location (e.g. flagship hospital or retail outlet).
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Penalty is not just cash! If lessee constructed heavy leasehold improvements with 10-year life on a 5-year lease, abandonment is an economic penalty, making renewal reasonably certain!

29. Reassessment of Lease Term (Ind AS 116 Para 20-21)

Mandatory Reassessment Triggers
Significant Event / Change in Circumstances: Entity SHALL reassess lease term upon occurrence of a significant event or change in circumstances that is within the control of the lessee and affects reasonable certainty.
Contractual Obligation: Lessee exercises an option not previously included, or does not exercise an option previously included.
What Does NOT Trigger Reassessment
External Market Changes: Fluctuations in market rent or prevailing interest rates outside the lessee's control do NOT trigger reassessment of options.
Mere Passage of Time: Time passing without any active operational or contractual change does not warrant term revision.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Reassessment of options requires LESSEE-CONTROLLED SIGNIFICANT ACTION (e.g. undertaking major leasehold improvements, acquiring adjacent businesses, altering corporate strategy). External market movements alone do NOT trigger reassessment!

32. Revision of Lease Term vs Reassessment of Options

Two Distinct Mechanisms of Term Modification
Mechanism 1: Reassessment of Discretionary Options: Lessee reassesses whether it is reasonably certain to exercise renewal/purchase or avoid termination due to internal events within its control.
Mechanism 2: Revision of Non-Cancellable Period: Occurs when the enforceable period itself changes due to contractual events, legal orders, or lessor exercising non-discretionary clauses.
Accounting Treatment on Revision
Discount Rate Update: Whenever lease term is reassessed, lessee MUST update the discount rate (use current incremental borrowing rate).
ROU Asset Adjustment: Remeasured lease liability difference is adjusted against carrying value of ROU asset (profit/loss only if ROU reduced to zero).
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Key Rule: Term change = Mandatory Discount Rate Revision! Re-measure remaining liability using NEW discount rate at change date, and adjust ROU Asset.

34. Distinguishing Leases from Executory Supply of Goods/Services

Comparative Distinction Matrix
Lease (Ind AS 116): Customer controls the underlying productive asset throughout the period. Capitalized on balance sheet as ROU Asset + Lease Liability.

37. Components of Lease Payments (Ind AS 116 Para 27)

The 5 Core Elements of Lease Payments
1. Fixed Payments (Less Incentives): Contractual periodic rents minus any lease incentives receivable from lessor.
2. In-Substance Fixed Payments: Payments structured as variable, but are unavoidable in economic substance.
3. Variable Payments Linked to Index / Rate: Payments tied to CPI, inflation index, or benchmark interest rates (e.g. MIBOR).
4. Purchase Option Exercise Price: Included IF lessee is reasonably certain to exercise the option.
5. Termination Penalties & RVG: Termination penalty (if lease term reflects termination) + amounts expected to be payable under Residual Value Guarantees (RVG).
What is Strictly EXCLUDED from Lease Liability
Performance / Usage Variable Payments: Rents based on % of sales or machine operating hours are expensed in P&L as incurred.
Uncommitted Optional Periods: Payments for periods beyond the assessed lease term.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Memory Code: [PAYMENTS = Fixed - Incentives + In-Substance Fixed + Index/Rate Variables + Probable Purchase Option + Expected RVG Outflow + Termination Penalty]. Pure sales % or machine hour variable rents NEVER enter initial lease liability!

38. In-Substance Fixed Lease Payments Deep Dive (Para B42)

Identifying Unavoidable Disguised Commitments
Clauses with No Genuine Commercial Substance: Payments that must be made regardless of whether the asset is operated (e.g. pay ₹1,000 if used, but ₹1,000 if idle).
Choice of Multiple Sets of Cash Flows: Lessee must select among several options, but at least one set is unavoidable (entity includes the set with the lowest present value).
Variable Payments with a Floor
Guaranteed Minimum Threshold: A clause stating "Rent is 2% of sales, subject to minimum ₹10 Lakhs/year" means ₹10 Lakhs is in-substance fixed.
Escalation Clauses: Rent increases by higher of 2% or inflation; the minimum 2% floor is in-substance fixed.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
ICAI Trap: Always isolate the minimum guaranteed baseline / floor. The floor amount is an in-substance fixed payment capitalized on Day 1. Only the excess over the floor is variable P&L expense!

43. Variable Lease Payments: Index/Rate vs Performance/Usage

The Fundamental Bifurcation
Category A: Linked to an Index or Benchmark Rate: Consumer Price Index (CPI), Wholesale Price Index (WPI), benchmark interest rate (LIBOR, MIBOR, SOFR). INCLUDED in Lease Liability.
Category B: Linked to Performance or Usage: % of revenue/turnover, units produced, distance travelled, kilowatt-hours generated. EXCLUDED from Lease Liability (expensed in P&L).
Initial Measurement vs Re-measurement Mechanics

Re-measurement Trigger: When index or interest rate changes and cash flows are revised, lease liability is re-measured.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Strict Mandate: Never forecast future inflation! Value Day 1 liability using the index as on commencement date. When the index resets at the end of the contracted cycle, re-measure liability and adjust ROU Asset!

47. Discount Rate: Rate Implicit in Lease vs Incremental Borrowing Rate

Hierarchy of Discount Rates (Ind AS 116 Para 26)
First Preference: Interest Rate Implicit in the Lease (IRIL): The rate that causes [PV of Lease Payments + PV of Unguaranteed Residual Value] to equal [Fair Value of Asset + Initial Direct Costs of Lessor].
Default Practical Alternative: Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR): Used if IRIL cannot be readily determined (which is almost always true for lessees because lessor's internal costs/residuals are proprietary).
Determinants of Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR)
Entity Specific: Credit standing and credit rating of the specific lessee entity.
Economic Environment: Term of lease, currency of payment, and nature/quality of underlying asset as security/collateral.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule

48. Initial Measurement of Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset (Para 24)

The 4-Component Cost Formula of ROU Asset
1. Initial Lease Liability: Present value of unpaid future lease payments.
2. Prepayments: Lease payments made at or before the commencement date (e.g. advance rent).
3. Initial Direct Costs (IDC): Incremental costs incurred by lessee to obtain lease (brokerage, legal fee, stamp duty).

Mandatory Deduction

The Two Golden Categories of Re-measurement

78. Lessor Lease Classification: 5 Primary & 3 Secondary Indicators

5 Primary Indicators of a Finance Lease (Para 63)
1. Title Transfer: Ownership transfers to lessee by the end of the lease term.
2. Bargain Purchase Option: Lessee has option to buy at price sufficiently lower than fair value, reasonably certain to exercise.
3. Major Part of Economic Life: Lease term is for the major part (generally $\ge$ 75%) of asset's economic life.
4. Substantially All Fair Value: At inception, PV of lease payments amounts to substantially all (generally $\ge$ 90%) of asset's fair value.
5. Specialized Asset: Asset is of such specialized nature that only lessee can use it without major modifications.
3 Secondary Indicators (Para 64)
6. Lessee Cancellation Loss: Lessee bears lessor's losses associated with cancellation.
7. Fluctuations in Residual Value: Gains/losses from residual value fluctuations accrue to lessee.
8. Secondary Period at Substantial Discount: Lessee can continue lease for secondary period at rent substantially below market.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Meeting ANY ONE of the 5 primary indicators strongly classifies the lease as a Finance Lease. If risks and rewards remain with lessor → Operating Lease!

Gross Investment in Lease (GIL): Sum of [Lease Payments receivable by lessor] + [Unguaranteed Residual Value (UGRV) accruing to lessor].
Net Investment in Lease (NIL): Gross Investment discounted at the interest rate implicit in the lease (IRIL).
Unearned Finance Income (UFI) & P&L Recognition
Formula: Unearned Finance Income (UFI) = Gross Investment - Net Investment.
Finance Income in P&L: Recognized over lease term based on a pattern reflecting a constant periodic rate of return on lessor's net investment.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Lessor Finance Lease Equations: GIL = Total Future Rents + UGRV. NIL = PV of GIL @ IRIL. UFI = GIL - NIL. Initial Direct Costs of non-manufacturer lessor are included in NIL and reduce finance income over time.

92. Sale and Leaseback Transactions: Ind AS 115 Control & Rights Framework

Step 1: Determine Whether Transfer is a 'Sale' under Ind AS 115
Transfer Qualifies as Sale (Performance Obligation Satisfied): Buyer obtains control of underlying asset. Apply Ind AS 116 sale & leaseback accounting.
Transfer Does NOT Qualify as Sale: Failed sale! Account as financing transaction under Ind AS 109 (Seller-lessee continues recognizing asset; cash received recognized as financial liability / loan).
Step 2: Accounting for a Qualifying Sale & Leaseback
ROU Asset Measurement: Seller-lessee measures ROU Asset at the proportion of previous carrying amount that relates to rights of use retained: ROU = Carrying Amount × [PV of Lease Payments / Fair Value of Asset].
Proportionate Gain/Loss in P&L: Seller-lessee recognizes gain/loss ONLY relating to rights transferred: Gain in P&L = Total Gain × [(Fair Value - PV of Lease Payments) / Fair Value].
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Never book 100% gain on sale & leaseback! You can ONLY recognize gain on the rights transferred to buyer. The gain on rights retained is deducted from the ROU Asset!

94. Legal Title vs Control in Sale and Leaseback (Ind AS 115 & 116)

Fundamental Principle: Control Governs, Legal Title Does Not
Control Transfer under Ind AS 115 is Decisive: Whether a transaction is a Sale and Leaseback depends solely on whether performance obligations under Ind AS 115 are satisfied by transferring control of the underlying asset to the buyer-lessor. Transfer of legal title or vehicle registration certificate (RC) is NOT determinative.
Sequence Test (Who Held Control First?): If lessee obtained control of the asset before control was transferred to the buyer-lessor, it is a Sale and Leaseback. If lessor obtained control directly from the supplier and then leased it to lessee, it is a pure two-party lease, even if legal registration was placed directly in lessee's name.
Detailed Practical Application Scenarios
Scenario A (Pure Lease via Third-Party Buyer): Entity needs a car. Friend purchases car from Tata Motors (friend acquires control). For ease of daily operations and local police checks, RC is registered in Entity's name. Friend leases car to Entity. Accounting: Ordinary lease under Ind AS 116 (Friend = Lessor, Entity = Lessee). Entity never had prior control, so no sale occurred.
Scenario B (True Sale & Leaseback): Entity purchases car from Tata Motors, obtains control and legal title, then sells it to Friend and leases it back. Accounting: Meets Ind AS 116 Sale & Leaseback criteria. Entity derecognizes carrying value proportion and recognizes gain strictly on rights transferred.
⚡ Exam Day Core Rule
Always verify: Did the seller-lessee possess control of the asset before transferring it to the buyer-lessor? If the buyer-lessor acquired control directly from an external vendor, the transaction is an ordinary lease under Ind AS 116, regardless of whose name appears on legal registration documents!

Universal Coverage: Applies to all contracts that convey the right to use an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration, including right-of-use assets in a sublease, applicable to both lessees and lessors.

5 Practical Expedients for Leases Previously Operating under Ind AS 17
1. Single Discount Rate for Portfolio: Lessee may apply a single discount rate to a portfolio of leases with reasonably similar characteristics (similar remaining lease term, similar class of asset, and similar economic environment).
2. The ROU asset is adjusted by the amount of the onerous lease provision.
3. Leases Ending within 12 Months: Lessee may elect not to apply Ind AS 116 to leases for which the lease term ends within 12 months of 1 April 2019 (i.e. expiring on or before 31 March 2020). These leases are accounted for as short-term leases (rentals expensed on SLM basis).
4. Exclusion of Initial Direct Costs: Lessee may exclude initial direct costs from the measurement of the ROU asset at 1 April 2019 under Option A.
5. Use of Hindsight: Lessee may use hindsight, such as in determining the lease term if the contract contains options to extend or terminate the lease.

Indian Carve-Outs from IFRS 16
1. Investment Property Accounting (Ind AS 40 vs IAS 40): Under IFRS 16 / IAS 40, a lessee can apply the Fair Value model to an ROU asset that meets the definition of investment property.
